Zoho is the source of truth. The store follows it automatically.

The automations

Two routines keep the store correct. Catalogue looks after what products are; Commerce looks after what they cost and how many are shown. You never trigger any of this.

Every morning · Catalogue, 07:07

  1. 1Image QueueDownloads the image links you pasted in the Fix sheet, checks each one against the product, uploads the good ones. Rejections get a written reason.
  2. 2Image search + auditHunts for missing product photos on manufacturer sites. Every found image is visually reviewed before it can touch the store.
  3. 3Change watchFinds every item you edited yesterday. A new photo or changed text on a live item is re-published the same run.
  4. 4Sync + verifyPublishes ready items: builds the product, groups variants, sets price and stock. Then checks the store to confirm each one is actually live.
  5. 5Reconcile + expiryCompares every live item to Zoho and corrects price and stock drift. Applies markdowns to short-dated goods. Pulls expired goods off the store.
  6. 6GalleriesPublishes the extra product photos from the Image 2 / Image 3 URL fields, after review.
  7. 7Fix TrackerWorks every Open and Reopened row in the Issues tab, writes what was done.
  8. 8ReportDaily summary, including anything that needs a human decision.

Every afternoon · Commerce, 13:07 and 13:42

  1. 113:07 · The midday checkRuns on its own, even with no computer open. Corrects store prices and stock against Zoho, applies expiry markdowns and short-dated tags, and pulls expired goods off the store. Fix a price in Zoho before one o'clock and the store has it the same afternoon.
  2. 213:42 · The Commerce reviewReads what the morning run and the midday check did, and reports anything needing attention: price and stock drift, expiry buckets, batch caps, items ready for cutover. If the midday check itself failed to run, the review notices and re-runs it.

The safety ceiling

If more than 50 price and stock corrections are ever pending at once, the automation stops and reports instead of fixing. A pile that big means something upstream broke, and pushing it to the store would spread the damage.

Your tasks, and what each one triggers

Five jobs. Everything else is automatic.

1 · Keep Zoho accurate

Prices, stock, photos, descriptions. Change them in Zoho the moment they change in reality.

Why

The store copies Zoho twice a day (07:07 and 13:07), and this pipeline is the only thing keeping store prices honest: Zoho's own Shopify connection never syncs prices. A photo you replace in Zoho is live the next day.

Open Zoho items ↗

2 · Log store problems in the Fix sheet (Issues tab)

One row per problem: date, SKU, type, what you saw. Done.

Why

The automation cannot see the store the way a customer does. Your row gets fixed the next morning, with notes on what was done. Still wrong? Set the row to Reopened: the system must then try a different approach, never the same one twice.

Open the Issues tab ↗

3 · Fill the Image Queue

Paste the exact product's image URL in column F. The suggested-source column tells you where to look.

Why

These items already defeated automatic search: about 9 in 10 automatic finds for them were the wrong product. You will find the right image in seconds, and every URL you provide teaches the system a trusted source. Good URL: manufacturer or distributor page, exact model, colour, shade and pack size, no watermarks.

Open the Image Queue ↗

4 · Tag expiring goods

Tick "Is Batch Item" on products that expire, and fill "Expiry Date" with the nearest expiry on the shelf.

Why

One tick switches on automatic markdowns and the expired-goods pull. Full detail on the Expiring Goods page.

Open Zoho items ↗

5 · Optional: give products a gallery

Paste manufacturer photo URLs into "Image 2 URL" and "Image 3 URL".

Why

Multi-photo listings sell better. Each URL is reviewed and published the next morning.

Open Zoho items ↗
Never do these
  • Don't use "Contains Variants" when creating items. Always Single Item, with SKU and price. Variants without SKUs are invisible to the automation.
  • Don't change SKU or name on a live item, except the cutover step on the Expiring Goods page. SKU is the thread holding Zoho, the store, and order history together.
  • Don't price items under ₦100. They are treated as placeholders and never publish.
  • Don't edit the store directly. It gets overwritten by the next automatic run.

Expiring goods

Selling expired dental materials is a compliance problem. Letting short-dated stock sit at full price is a financial one. This workflow solves both automatically.

What happens once an item is tagged

180+ days
Full price
91 to 180 days
10% off
31 to 90 days
25% off
30 days or less
40% off
Expired
Pulled off the store

Customers see the discount with the full price struck through. The discount never goes below what we paid for the item; goods that hit that floor are flagged for disposal review. Zoho's selling price is never touched: discounts exist only on the store.

Several batches on the shelf? The store sells one batch at a time, oldest first. The listing shows only the soonest batch's quantity, at that batch's discounted price, and gets a "short-dated" tag. Fresh stock stays hidden until the old batch sells out; the next automatic check flips the listing to the fresh batch at its own price and the tag comes off. Nobody ever buys fresh stock at the old batch's discount, and the discount does its real job: clearing the short-dated units first.

The transition to true batch tracking

Today, expiry is one hand-filled date per item. The destination is Zoho batch tracking, where every delivery carries its own expiry automatically. Zoho cannot convert an existing item once it has history, so we replace items one by one, with zero effect on the store:

Tag everything that expires now

Tick Is Batch Item, fill Expiry Date. Markdown protection is live immediately, before any migration.

New items are born batch-tracked now

Any new expiring product is created with batch tracking on. Expiry then records itself with every delivery.

Cut over just before a resupply arrives rolling

When a tagged item's stock drops to its reorder level, the daily report lists it as cutover ready: that is the moment, because the shelf is at its lowest and easiest to count. Count the remaining units per expiry date, rename the old item's name and SKU with a "-X" ending (one edit), and the automation rebuilds it batch-tracked under the original SKU with those units as its first batches. Then receive the new delivery into the rebuilt item, entering the batch number and expiry at the receiving step. Every reorder migrates an item. The store never notices: same page, same order history.

Retire the hand-filled date later

When every expiring item is batch-tracked, the Expiry Date field is no longer needed.

Common questions

We have several batches with different expiry dates. Which one sets the discount?
The store sells one batch at a time, oldest first. The listing shows only the soonest batch's quantity, at that batch's discounted price. When those units sell out, the next automatic check flips the listing to the next batch: its quantity, its price. Until an item is batch-tracked, the hand-filled Expiry Date field plays the same role for the whole shelf: keep it set to the nearest expiry, and update it when that stock sells out.
Only two short-dated units are left, but we have a hundred fresh ones. Does everything get discounted?
No. The listing shows quantity 2 at the discount; the hundred fresh units are hidden until those two sell. Nobody can buy fresh stock at the short-dated price. When you ship those orders, pick the short-dated batch: it is the one the customer paid the discount for. If a tiny discounted batch lingers unsold and is blocking fresh stock, the daily report flags it so you can sell those units offline and unblock the listing.
We reorder before stock ever reaches zero. When do we cut over?
Just before the resupply arrives. The daily report flags a tagged item the moment its stock drops to its reorder level. The shelf is at its lowest then, so counting the leftover units per expiry date takes minutes. Rename the old item with "-X", the automation rebuilds it batch-tracked with those units, and you receive the new delivery into the rebuilt item with its batch and expiry. Every reorder migrates one more item.
What if some units on the shelf have already expired but we also have fresh stock?
The listing stays live on the fresh batches (expired units are never shown or counted on the store). The expired units are flagged in the daily report to be removed from sellable stock and disposed of. A listing only comes off the store when everything on the shelf is expired.
How can we tell on the store that an item is short-dated?
Two ways, both automatic: the price shows a discount with the full price struck through, and the product carries a "short-dated" tag. The tag comes off by itself at the next automatic check after the short-dated batch sells out. The tag also makes it possible to show a "Short-dated deals" collection page later, with zero extra work.
Does a discount change our price in Zoho?
Never. Zoho's selling price is untouched; the discount exists only on the store, shown with the full price struck through. Fix the price in Zoho and the store follows at the next automatic check, discount recalculated on top.
A customer orders a discounted item. Which units do we ship?
The oldest batch first. When you raise the invoice, Zoho lists the batches sorted first-expiring-first; pick from the top. That is what the discount exists to do: move the short-dated stock before it expires.
Can the discount ever sell below what we paid?
No. The price floors at the item's purchase cost. Items that hit the floor are flagged for disposal review, because at that point holding them is costing more than clearing them.
